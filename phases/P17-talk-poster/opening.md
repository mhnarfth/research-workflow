HOW TO WORK THIS:

1. THE ONE MESSAGE. What is the single thing the audience should leave with?
   One sentence. Everything else serves it or gets cut. Propose two or three
   candidates and argue for one.

<IF TALK:>
2. STRUCTURE backward from that message. Slide by slide: what's on it, what I
   say, roughly how long. Target 80% of my slot — overrunning means the
   conclusion gets rushed and Q&A eats it.
3. FIGURES. Which paper figures work on a projector and which don't? For each
   one used, what needs to change: font size, element count, contrast, line
   weight. I restyle from figdata JSONs, so tell me the knob values, not new
   plots.
4. SPEAKER TRANSCRIPT. Write what I'll actually say, not bullet summaries. I
   need something to fall back on when nervous.
5. Q&A PREP. From the attached attack lists, the top 3 questions I'll be asked
   and a crisp answer to each. Do not be defensive in these — the honest
   answer is usually stronger.

<IF POSTER:>
2. LAYOUT. One visual focal point, then supporting elements. What goes where,
   and what gets cut entirely. People scan posters; they don't read them.
3. FIGURES. Which one is the focal point, and what needs to change for it to
   read from two meters?
4. THE 60-SECOND PITCH. Write what I say when someone stops at the poster.
   This is what actually gets used; the poster is the prop.
5. Q&A PREP — same as above.

COACHING: teach me inline where something comes up that I may be reading wrong.
