# P17 — Talk or Poster

Lighter manual — expand it the first time you present.

## Talk

**One message.** Decide the single thing the audience should leave with, then
structure backward from it. Everything that doesn't serve it is a candidate cut.

**Restyle the figures for projection** — bigger fonts, fewer elements, higher
contrast, no thin lines. Your `figdata` JSONs make this trivial: a **third
styling profile** alongside print, in `analysis/restyle_figures.py`. No
retraining, no digging up source data.

**Write the transcript**, don't just make slides. It's what makes timing real
and it's what you fall back on when nervous.

**Time it, then cut to 80% of the slot.** Overrunning means your conclusion
gets rushed and Q&A eats it.

**Prepare answers to the top 3 attacks from P11.** You already know what they
are — that's the point of having run it. The questions you'll get are the ones
the adversarial review already found.

## Poster

**One visual focal point.** Minimal text. Readable from two meters. People scan
posters; they don't read them.

**A 60-second verbal pitch** is what actually gets used — write it and practice
it. The poster is the prop.

## Both

Have a one-sentence answer to "what did you find?" ready before you arrive.
