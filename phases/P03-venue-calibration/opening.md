PHASE: Venue calibration

WHAT I'M DECIDING: target venue and fallback ladder, how this venue writes, and
the reverse schedule.

REQUIRED CONTEXT: FEASIBILITY.md, the chosen idea (P02 handoff)
ATTACHMENTS: the CFP; 2-3 recent papers from the target venue

WHAT I WANT OUT: a filled-in VENUE.md — ladder, style observations, exemplars,
preprint policy, artifact-evaluation check, and a computed schedule with dates.

---

MY ANSWERS FIRST:

Target venue: <...>
Why this audience, specifically: <...>
Today's date: <YYYY-MM-DD>   Submission deadline: <YYYY-MM-DD>

---

HOW TO WORK THIS:

1. LADDER. Build target / up / down / never.
   - Choose by the NATURE of the contribution, not the amount of material. A
     simulated system is not a systems paper no matter how many pages it runs.
   - The NEVER line matters as much as the target: name venues that
     structurally don't fit and say why, so I can't be talked into them later.
   - "Up" must be conditional on a specific result landing, not on vague hope.

2. STYLE. From the attached papers, tell me: page limit and what's excluded;
   prose style (continuous vs. bulleted); what counts as a contribution here;
   the typical evidence bar; appendix rules; preprint policy; whether artifact
   evaluation runs; this year's theme; which CFP topic numbers I hit.
   Then name 2 of the attached papers as style exemplars — I'll attach those
   again at drafting.

3. SCHEDULE. Compute concrete dates from today and the deadline using these
   ratios of the runway: setup 20%, execution 40%, writing 25%, review 8%,
   buffer 5%. The submission buffer has a hard floor of 24 hours. Give me a
   dated table.

Format the schedule as a table with columns: block | ends | date | what must be
done by then.

Do not tell me to cut scope. If the runway looks tight, say so and show me the
numbers; the scope decision is mine.

COACHING: As we work, teach me inline — briefly, where it comes up, not saved
for the end. If a metric, plot, statistic, or convention appears that I may be
reading wrong, say what it means and which direction is good. If I state
something incorrect, correct me directly rather than working around it. Keep it
to a line or two each time; don't turn the session into a lecture.
