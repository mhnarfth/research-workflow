PHASE: Paper planning

WHAT I'M DECIDING: the section-by-section structure against the page limit, and
every number the paper will report.

REQUIRED CONTEXT: the execution debrief (P07 handoff), RESEARCH-PLAN.md,
VENUE.md, LITERATURE.md, NOTATION.md
ATTACHMENTS: all metric tables and figures produced; the draft abstract from P05

WHAT I WANT OUT: SECTION-PLAN.md with word budgets, and a complete CLAIMS.md.

---

MY ANSWERS FIRST:

The claim, one sentence, as it now stands after seeing the results: <...>
Which figures I think should ship: <...>
Has the finding changed from what I planned? <...>

---

HOW TO WORK THIS:

1. SECTION PLAN. Against the page limit and prose style in VENUE.md, give me a
   table: section | pages | WORD BUDGET | what it contains | which figures and
   tables appear | which contribution it delivers.
   Word budgets, not just page counts — that's what keeps drafting overshoot
   manageable.

2. FIGURE AND TABLE SELECTION. From everything produced, which ships? For each
   one cut, say why. A figure that doesn't carry a claim doesn't ship.

3. CLAIMS LEDGER. List EVERY number the paper will report — p-values,
   percentages, runtimes, parameter counts, dataset sizes, everything — with the
   exact artifact file each comes from. If a number I've mentioned doesn't
   trace to a file, flag it rather than including it.

4. HONEST-FRAMING CHECK. Run every claim against these:
   - Is a difference inside noise being reported as an improvement?
   - Is "not significantly different" being stated as "equivalent"? That needs
     an equivalence test, not a null result.
   - Is any aggregate driven by one unit? If so, report mean AND median and
     name the unit.
   - Is any correlation possibly a between-group artifact?
   - Is any metric name ambiguous against my other papers?
   Tell me which claims need weakening, specifically and with the wording.

5. NOTATION. Check against NOTATION.md and flag conflicts.

6. ABSTRACT COMPARISON. Compare the P05 draft abstract with what actually
   happened. Where they differ is often the most interesting thing here — say
   what changed and whether the real finding is a better paper than the planned
   one.

COACHING: As we work, teach me inline — briefly, where it comes up, not saved
for the end. If a metric, plot, statistic, or convention appears that I may be
reading wrong, say what it means and which direction is good. If I state
something incorrect, correct me directly rather than working around it. Keep it
to a line or two each time; don't turn the session into a lecture.
