WHAT I'M DECIDING: the research questions, the contributions I'll claim, and
the figures and tables that would prove them.

REQUIRED CONTEXT: LITERATURE.md, FEASIBILITY.md, VENUE.md, NOTATION.md
ATTACHMENTS: references.bib

WHAT I WANT OUT: a RESEARCH-PLAN.md with research questions, claimed
contributions, figure and table sketches, and a draft abstract.

---

MY ANSWERS FIRST, BEFORE YOU WEIGH IN:

Research questions: <...>
Contributions I intend to claim: <...>
The one-sentence claim: <...>

---

HOW TO WORK THIS:

1. RESEARCH QUESTIONS. Attack mine. Is each one actually answerable with the
   data and budget in FEASIBILITY.md? Is each one a question a reviewer at this
   venue would care about? Cut any that isn't.

2. CONTRIBUTIONS. Phrase them in this venue's idiom (see VENUE.md). For each,
   tell me what evidence would be required to support it and what would falsify
   it. A contribution I can't falsify isn't a contribution.

3. FIGURE AND TABLE SKETCHES — the important part. Sketch the 5-7 figures and
   3-4 tables that would PROVE the claim. For each, give:
   - number and working title
   - what it shows, concretely (axes, grouping, what the reader should see)
   - which contribution it supports
   - what it would look like if the claim were FALSE
   That last one matters: a figure that looks the same either way proves
   nothing.

   These become the target the pipeline is built to hit. Treat them as a
   target, not a fixed contract — new figures will appear during execution.

4. DRAFT ABSTRACT. Write the abstract I hope to write, at this venue's typical
   length. It will be wrong. Write it anyway — I need the claim named before
   I'm invested in a result.

5. NOTATION. Check my symbols against NOTATION.md. Flag any quantity where I'd
   be introducing a second symbol for something I've already named in a prior
   paper.

Give me the strongest case against my RQs and contributions, not encouragement.

COACHING: As we work, teach me inline — briefly, where it comes up, not saved
for the end. If a metric, plot, statistic, or convention appears that I may be
reading wrong, say what it means and which direction is good. If I state
something incorrect, correct me directly rather than working around it. Keep it
to a line or two each time; don't turn the session into a lecture.
