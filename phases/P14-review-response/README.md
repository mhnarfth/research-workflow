# P14 — Review Response

Two paths. They're really different phases sharing a trigger.

## Path A — Revise / rebut

**In:** the reviews · the paper · `CLAIMS.md` (**as submitted** — that's what
reviewers read) · `NOTES.md`
**Out:** response document · revision plan · revised paper

Work each review point into: agree and fix · agree and concede · respectfully
disagree with evidence · already addressed and we'll point to it. Every
"disagree" needs evidence, not assertion.

Your advisor sees the finished response, not the drafting of it — that's how
you already work.

## Path B — Rejected

Harder, and it has a failure mode with a name: **a rejected paper sitting
idle.** So the path is explicit:

1. **Extract what's real** from the reviews, separately from what's noise.
   Do this after a day or two, not the hour they arrive.
2. **Decide what changes** — and whether any change needs code
3. **Pick the next venue from the ladder already in `VENUE.md`.** This is why
   the ladder exists. You made that decision calmly, months ago
4. **Recompute the schedule** for the new deadline, same ratios
5. **Archive the reviews** — venue B's reviewers will find what venue A found

## Archive either way

`research-index/reviews/<venue><year>.md`: the reviews, your response, and what
you changed.

Three uses:
- **Pre-emptively**, at the next paper's P11 — attach `PATTERNS.md` and ask
  whether the new draft is vulnerable to the same objections
- **On resubmission** — B will find what A found unless you addressed it
- **As a pattern, yearly** — three reviews saying "the evaluation is on a proxy
  metric" isn't three difficult reviewers, it's a gap in how you design studies

`PATTERNS.md` gets distilled once a year, and it's what you attach — not the
full archive.
