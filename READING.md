# Reading List

Track status in `LEDGER-reading.md`.

## Start here — highest value per hour
- Wilson et al., **Good Enough Practices in Scientific Computing**, PLOS Comp Bio 2017 — deliberately pragmatic: not what's ideal, what's achievable
- **The Turing Way** — book.the-turing-way.org — a reference to dip into, not read cover to cover

## Project organization
- Noble, *A Quick Guide to Organizing Computational Biology Projects*, PLOS Comp Bio 2009 — the origin of the layout everyone now uses
- Sandve et al., *Ten Simple Rules for Reproducible Computational Research*, PLOS Comp Bio 2013
- Cookiecutter Data Science — drivendata.github.io

## ML-specific
- Pineau et al., *Improving Reproducibility in Machine Learning Research*, JMLR 2021 — where the NeurIPS reproducibility checklist came from
- Karpathy, *A Recipe for Training Neural Networks* (blog, 2019) — the best practical writeup on not fooling yourself with models
- ACM Artifact Review and Badging v1.1 — read before building, not after

## Writing
- Simon Peyton Jones, *How to Write a Great Research Paper* (talk)
- Mensh & Kording, *Ten Simple Rules for Structuring Papers*, PLOS Comp Bio 2017
- Whitesides, *Writing a Paper*, Advanced Materials 2004 — two pages; argues for outlining into the figures before running experiments
- Silvia, *How to Write a Lot* — short; about the scheduling problem, not the prose problem

## The PhD
- Philip Guo, *The Ph.D. Grind* — free memoir, honest about how nonlinear research actually is
